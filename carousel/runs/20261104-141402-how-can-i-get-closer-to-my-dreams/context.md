# Run context — "how can I get closer to my dreams" (topic-scoped)

## 1. Topic & angle
- **Topic:** how can I get closer to my dreams.
- **Angle:** "The Someday Gap" — the distance to a dream is rarely a wanting
  problem; it's a scheduling problem. Pure daydreaming can even backfire
  (the brain tastes the win and coasts), while one concrete *when-then* moves
  the dream from mood to calendar. Counterintuitive core: dreaming harder is
  not the lever — booking the dream is.
- **Why this angle:** every generic version says "chase your dreams" or
  "stay motivated"; none names the mechanism of why wishing stalls and what
  actually moves a dream (closure + a time slot). Mirror of the activation
  brand: reframe, mechanism, small physical move.

## 2. Audience of this topic
- Dreamers with a stalled "someday": side-project apostles, aspiring writers/
  creators, career changers, fitness or travel "one day" people. Problem-aware:
  they feel the gap (pins saved, notebooks full, nothing started) but blame
  discipline or timing.
- Want: proof they're not lazy; a way to start that survives motivation dips.
- Fear: becoming someone who only ever talks about the dream.
- Tried: vision boards, inspiration folders, motivational videos, "this year
  I will" — all fantasy-first, all stalled.

## 3. Call-out & targeting
- Opening call-out (proposed, proven pattern from the productivity lane):
  name the reader's blame reflex, hand back the reframe — "You don't lack
  *dreams*. You lack a next step."
- Moment call-outs to use in this lane's sets: the saved-inspiration folder,
  the Sunday-evening "this is the year" mood, the goal restarted every
  January, the daydream on the commute.
- Vocabulary to reuse verbatim: someday, dream, next step, daydream,
  "when the time is right", vision board, one day, I'll start, booked.

## 4. Ban list (anti-neutralization for this-topic sets)
- "Chase your dreams", "never give up", "5 habits of successful dreamers",
  "manifest it", hustle-poster framing, vague "believe in yourself".
- No generic "your day/life" placeholders — every slice stays dream-anchored
  (the gym-at-January mood, the novel at page zero).
- Test: if a slide could post unchanged from any niche, the angle is lost —
  rewrite until the stalled dreamer feels named in line one.

## 5. Arc + facts (verified before use)
hook (reframe: not a want gap, a step gap) → proof (vivid fantasizers put in
less effort and achieved less — Oettingen & Mayer, 2002, Person. Soc. Psych.
Bull.) → mechanism (an imagined win reads as tasted; the engine idles — same
source) → recognition (the saved-inspiration folder is *someday* in disguise)
→ counterintuitive fix (one *when-then* on paper: "Tomorrow, 6 a.m., I
write" — Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006, Adv. Exp. Soc. Psych., meta-analysis of
94 studies) → CTA (dreams don't need louder wants; they need **appointments**).
- Follow-up angles in this lane: vision boards vs mental contrasting,
  the "first ugly session", dream checkpoints, why inspiration folders stall,
  restarting a dead goal.
- Numbers inside plain sentences only ("a meta-analysis of 94 studies").
