# Run context — topic-scoped (do NOT apply to other topics)

## 1. Topic & angle
- **Topic:** how to stay productive at work.
- **Angle:** "The Switching Tax" — the trait readers blame (being unfocused)
  is reframed as environmental: fragmented work. Workday output isn't lost to
  laziness, it's lost to switches without closure. Contrast: generic advice
  says "do more/morning routines"; this angle says **finishing beats doing**.
  Mechanism: attention residue (Leroy, 2009), fragmentation (Mark et al.,
  2005), 23-minute refocus cost (Gloria Mark, UC Irvine).

## 2. Audience of this topic
- Knowledge workers / office professionals: desk job, email, chat, meetings,
  tabs. Problem-aware — they feel "busy all day, shipped little" but don't
  know the mechanism.
- Want: end the day feeling finished, in control.
- Fear: wasted effort, looking busy while delivering little, burnout guilt.
- Tried: longer hours, todo apps, motivational pushes — didn't stick, because
  none addressed switching itself.

## 3. Call-out & targeting
- Opening call-out (proven this run): "You're not *unfocused*. Your workday
  is just switching tasks all day." — names the reader's self-blame, then
  hands them the reframe (value condition).
- Vocabulary to reuse verbatim in THIS topic's hooks/subtitles: workday,
  inbox, tabs, pings, meetings, deep work, "busy all day", "nothing done",
  interruptions, refocus.
- Address the reader as "you/your workday" — moment-based, never "everyone".

## 4. Ban list (anti-neutralization for this-topic sets)
- "10 productivity tips", "pro tips for productivity", "level up your day",
  "hacks successful people use", vague hustle framing.
- Defense-fragment tricks and "your day" placeholders — they neutralize the
  work context. Keep every slice work-anchored.
- generic: if a slide could post unchanged from any niche, rewrite until the
  knowledge worker feels named in line one.

## 5. Arc + facts (this-topic proven arc; reuse only across productivity-lane sets)
hook (reframe: the trait they blame is a system flaw) → proof (a new task
starts every **3 minutes** — Mark et al., 2005) → mechanism (every switch
leaves residue; half the mind **stays behind** — Leroy, 2009) → cost (it can
take **23 minutes** to refocus — Gloria Mark, UC Irvine) → counterintuitive
fix (never switch work tasks empty-handed: jot the **next step**) →
CTA (productivity at work isn't doing more; it's **finishing**; one-commitment
ask).
- Follow-up angles in this lane only: morning single-tasking, meeting
  fragmentation, end-of-day shutdown ritual, notification hygiene,
  "finish list" vs to-do list.
